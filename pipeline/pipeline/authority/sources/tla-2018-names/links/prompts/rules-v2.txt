An Egyptian personal name is a sequence of words: names of gods, nouns, adjectives, verbs, prepositions, particles and pronouns. In a TLA transliteration, hyphens and spaces separate words, a dot marks off an ending (such as a feminine .t or a plural .w), and = attaches a suffix pronoun; the translations render the name in modern spelling. A modern spelling such as record 1 renders the same words with vowels added by convention, often written as one word with no separators.

Record 1 and a candidate are the same name when record 1 can be read as the candidate's words, in the same order, by these conventions:
- each consonant sign is rendered by its own letter or letters: ḥ as h; ḫ and ẖ as kh or ch; š as sh; ṯ as tj or t; ḏ as dj or d; z as s; q and ḳ as q or k; every other consonant by its own letter;
- ꜣ and ꜥ are rendered as a vowel or not at all; the weak consonants j (also written ꞽ or i̯), y and w are rendered as i, y, u, w or not at all;
- vowel letters may be added anywhere and stand for no sign;
- apart from what the lines above allow, record 1 may not render a consonant the candidate lacks, nor leave out a consonant the candidate has; the one further omission allowed is a feminine ending t.
Matching consonants are necessary but not enough: the candidate's translations show which words it is made of, and the candidate is a different name when record 1, read as a modern spelling, renders different words (for instance, a different word that happens to share consonants).
Who bore a name is not evidence that two spellings are one name.
